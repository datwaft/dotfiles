# Safety-instruction protocol

Read this reference before writing or rewriting a warning, caution, hazard,
safety notice, or protective action.

## Official Issue 9 basis

ASD-STE100 Section 7 contains three rules:

- **7.1:** Identify the applicable risk level.
- **7.2:** Start with a clear and accurate command or condition.
- **7.3:** Explain the risk or possible result.

In the aerospace and defense definitions used by Issue 9:

- A **warning** identifies a risk of injury or death.
- A **caution** identifies a risk of damage to objects.

Other industries can use different signal words and severity systems. Preserve
the governing domain’s label and policy.

## Safety-preservation gate

Extract these elements from the source before editing:

- Signal word or severity category.
- Hazardous condition or source.
- Exposed person, object, or system.
- Required or prohibited action.
- Timing and placement relative to the task.
- Stated consequence and severity.
- Threshold, quantity, duration, probability, and exception.

Do not infer a missing element. In particular:

- Do not turn an ignition statement into an injury-or-death claim without a
  source basis.
- Do not change a caution into a warning merely because the hazard sounds
  serious.
- Do not add personal protective equipment, a shutdown step, an inspection, or
  a recovery action without authorization.
- Do not weaken `will cause`, `can cause`, or `may cause` into another level of
  certainty.
- Do not remove repeated hazard language when repetition establishes where the
  risk applies.

Ask the user or consult the governing safety source when the supplied severity
or consequence is insufficient to select a label.

## Construction pattern

When the source supports all elements, use this order:

1. Signal word or governing risk identifier.
2. Condition that the reader must know before acting, if applicable.
3. Clear command or prohibition.
4. Stated risk or possible result.

Keep each safety sentence within the procedural limit of 20 words. Use a
vertical list when necessary. Do not hide an instruction in a note.

## Review

Compare the source and rewrite for:

- Same signal word or an authorized equivalent.
- Same hazard and affected entity.
- Same protective action and timing.
- Same consequence and severity.
- Same probability, conditions, thresholds, and exceptions.
- No new claim and no omitted claim.

If safety accuracy conflicts with sentence length or controlled vocabulary,
keep the accurate content and report the unresolved rule conflict.
