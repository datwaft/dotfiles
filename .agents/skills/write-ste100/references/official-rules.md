# ASD-STE100 Issue 9 rule map

This reference paraphrases the 53 writing rules in ASD-STE100 Issue 9
(2025-01-15). It preserves the official section and rule numbering but does not
replace the specification. Use the current official document as the authority:
<https://www.asd-ste100.org/assets/files/ASD-STE100_ISSUE9.pdf>.

## 1. Words

- **1.1 — Permitted vocabulary:** Use a dictionary-approved word, a valid
  technical noun, or a valid technical verb.
- **1.2 — Part of speech:** Use a dictionary word only as its specified part of
  speech.
- **1.3 — Meaning:** Use a dictionary word only with its approved meaning.
- **1.4 — Forms:** Use only the approved forms of verbs and adjectives.
- **1.5 — Technical-noun categories:** Permit a word as a technical noun only
  when it belongs to an allowed technical-noun category.
- **1.6 — Unapproved words:** Use a word absent from the dictionary only when it
  is a valid technical noun or part of one.
- **1.7 — Technical nouns as verbs:** Do not turn a technical noun into a verb.
- **1.8 — Controlled terminology:** Prefer technical nouns already approved by
  the applicable company, industry, or subject field.
- **1.9 — New technical nouns:** When selecting a new technical noun, make it
  short and easy to understand.
- **1.10 — Register:** Do not use regional language, slang, or jargon as a
  technical noun.
- **1.11 — Term consistency:** Use one technical noun for one item throughout
  the text.
- **1.12 — Technical-verb categories:** Permit a technical verb only when it
  belongs to an allowed technical-verb category.
- **1.13 — Technical verbs as nouns:** Do not turn a technical verb into a noun.
- **1.14 — Spelling:** Use American English unless another governing directive
  requires different spelling.

## 2. Multi-word nouns

- **2.1 — Length:** Keep a multi-word noun to no more than three words.
- **2.2 — Official long terms:** When an established technical noun exceeds
  three words, write it in full at first use. Then define a clear short form or
  carefully hyphenate directly related words as one unit.

Do not alter an official technical noun merely to meet Rule 2.1. Follow Rule
2.2 and preserve the controlled term.

## 3. Verbs

- **3.1 — Dictionary forms:** Use only verb forms supplied by the dictionary.
- **3.2 — Permitted forms and tenses:** Use the infinitive, imperative, simple
  present, simple past, simple future, or a permitted past participle used as an
  adjective.
- **3.3 — Past participles:** Use a permitted past participle as an adjective to
  describe a condition, not as an unapproved verb construction.
- **3.4 — Complex constructions:** Do not use auxiliary verbs to construct
  unapproved compound or progressive verb forms.
- **3.5 — “-ing” forms:** Use an “-ing” form only as a technical noun or as a
  modifier inside a technical noun.
- **3.6 — Voice:** Use active voice. In descriptive writing, use passive voice
  only when the agent is unknown.
- **3.7 — Actions:** Express an action with an approved verb, not with a noun or
  another part of speech.

## 4. Sentences

- **4.1 — Clarity:** Write short sentences with an unambiguous structure.
- **4.2 — Completeness:** Do not omit required words or use contractions merely
  to shorten a sentence.
- **4.3 — Vertical lists:** Put complex material into a vertical list when that
  structure makes the relationships clear.
- **4.4 — Connections:** Use explicit connecting words or phrases between
  sentences with related topics.
- **4.5 — Determiners:** Use an applicable article (`a`, `an`, `the`) or
  demonstrative adjective (`this`, `these`) before a noun or multi-word noun.

## 5. Procedural writing

- **5.1 — Sentence length:** Use no more than 20 words in each procedural or
  safety sentence.
- **5.2 — Instructions:** Put one instruction in each sentence. Multiple
  actions can share a sentence only when they occur at the same time.
- **5.3 — Imperative:** Write instructions in command form.
- **5.4 — Conditions:** When the reader must know a condition first, put the
  descriptive condition before the command and separate them with a comma.
- **5.5 — Notes:** Put information in notes; do not put instructions,
  requirements, or limits in notes.

## 6. Descriptive writing

- **6.1 — Information rate:** Present information gradually.
- **6.2 — Logical structure:** Repeat useful key words or phrases to make the
  structure explicit.
- **6.3 — Sentence length:** Use no more than 25 words in each descriptive
  sentence.
- **6.4 — Paragraphs:** Use paragraphs to group related information.
- **6.5 — Paragraph topic:** Give each paragraph only one topic.
- **6.6 — Paragraph length:** Use no more than six sentences in a paragraph.

Descriptive text gives information rather than instructions. Do not use the
imperative merely to shorten descriptive content.

## 7. Safety instructions

- **7.1 — Risk identifier:** Use the applicable risk-level word or symbol.
- **7.2 — Opening:** Start with a clear, accurate command or condition.
- **7.3 — Consequence:** Explain the risk or possible result.

For the official aerospace and defense definitions, a warning concerns possible
injury or death; a caution concerns possible damage to objects. Other domains
can use different labels. Read `safety-instructions.md` before editing hazards.

## 8. Punctuation and word count

- **8.1 — Punctuation:** Use standard English punctuation except the semicolon.
- **8.2 — Hyphens:** Use hyphens to connect words that operate as one directly
  related unit.
- **8.3 — Parentheses:** Use parentheses only for the specified purposes:
  references; item identifiers; work-step identifiers; abbreviations;
  simultaneous singular/plural forms; explanations; or alternatives.
- **8.4 — Lists:** In a vertical list, treat the colon before the list as a
  sentence boundary for word counting. Count every list item as a new sentence.
- **8.5 — Parenthetical text:** Count all text inside one pair of parentheses as
  one word in its containing sentence.
- **8.6 — One-word elements:** Count each number, number-plus-unit,
  abbreviation, alphanumeric identifier, quoted text, title or label, and
  applicable proper noun as one word.
- **8.7 — Hyphenation:** Count a hyphenated unit as one word.

Word-count rules do not authorize deletion of necessary technical content.

## 9. Writing practices

- **9.1 — Reconstruction:** When direct word substitution produces an unclear or
  incorrect sentence, use a different sentence construction.
- **9.2 — Correct usage:** Use every approved word according to its dictionary
  entry, including its meaning, part of speech, and restrictions.
- **9.3 — Phrasal verbs:** Do not combine approved words into an unapproved
  phrasal verb.
- **9.4 — Consistency:** Use consistent terminology and wording.

## General recommendations

The eight general recommendations support clarity but are not part of the 53
numbered rules:

- **GR-1 — `that`:** Include or omit `that` according to grammar and clarity;
  do not create a second possible parse.
- **GR-2 — `with`:** Check what `with` modifies. Rewrite when it can express an
  unclear tool, condition, accompaniment, possession, or manner.
- **GR-3 — Pronouns:** Make every pronoun reference unambiguous.
- **GR-4 — `this`:** Repeat the noun when `this` could refer to more than one
  preceding item or event.
- **GR-5 — False friends:** Check words that resemble a word in another language
  but carry a different English meaning.
- **GR-6 — Latin abbreviations:** Prefer clear English to forms such as `e.g.`,
  `i.e.`, and `etc.`.
- **GR-7 — Inclusive language:** Use accurate, respectful, inclusive terms.
- **GR-8 — Possessives:** Select possessive constructions that remain clear and
  grammatically correct.

## Compliance boundary

This rule map cannot establish dictionary compliance. The controlled dictionary
is an equal part of STE. Use `dictionary-workflow.md` and the official Issue 9
specification before making lexical-compliance claims.
